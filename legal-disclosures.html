Legal & Editorial Disclosures

The People vs. Lee Harvey Oswald

The Official Record: The People of the United States vs. Lee Harvey Oswald is a work of historical fiction and speculative dramatization. While the “Rule of 1965” mandates the use of authentic witness testimony from the 26 volumes of the Warren Commission and contemporary 1960s evidence, the trial itself and the specific legal proceedings depicted never occurred.

Legal Notice: This podcast is for entertainment and educational purposes only. The dialogue for the court proceedings, judge’s rulings, and attorney arguments are fictionalized for the narrative. All opinions expressed by fictionalized versions of historical figures do not necessarily reflect the views of the actual persons or their estates. References to real-world experts, consultants, or archives are used to provide historical context and do not constitute an endorsement of the fictionalized “verdict” reached in this series.

Assigned Voice Disclaimer: The voices used in this production for certain historical figures, including witnesses and secondary participants, are artistic voice assignments generated using AI technology. These voices are not intended to replicate or impersonate the actual speaking voices of the individuals, and in many cases no authentic recordings exist. They are used solely to bring written historical testimony to life within this dramatized mock trial.

THE RULE OF 1965 EVIDENCE STANDARD

“The Rule of 1965 Evidence Standard: All courtroom evidence, testimony, records, photographs, recordings, and forensic knowledge must have tangibly existed on or before March 1, 1965. Material need not have been public, but it must have been reasonably obtainable by counsel through the legal and investigative methods available at the time. Qualifying preexisting material discovered during the reconstructed proceedings may be offered through May 15, 1965, subject to verification, authentication, admissibility, and the Court’s closing of the evidentiary record. Modern archives may be used only to locate contemporaneous materials. Post-1965 evidence, science, and interpretation are excluded from the courtroom.”
