1. Historical-Fiction Statement

The People vs. Lee Harvey Oswald is a work of historical fiction and speculative dramatization. While the production draws on authentic testimony, records, and evidence from 1965 and earlier, the trial itself never occurred. Lee Harvey Oswald did not survive the shooting by Jack Ruby, was never indicted, and was never tried. All courtroom proceedings, verdicts, and outcomes depicted in this series are fictional.

2. Rule of 1965 Methodology

This production follows a self-imposed evidentiary standard we call the Rule of 1965: all courtroom evidence, testimony, records, photographs, recordings, and forensic knowledge used in the series must have existed, or been reasonably obtainable through the investigative methods available, on or before March 1, 1965. Material developed after that date may inform our research process but is not presented as courtroom evidence unless it can be traced back to a source that independently satisfies this standard.

3. Verbatim Testimony vs. Dramatized Dialogue

Certain content in this production is drawn directly from the historical record, including sworn testimony, depositions, and public statements, and is presented as close to verbatim as the format allows. Other content, including courtroom procedure, attorney arguments, judicial rulings, and connective dialogue, is dramatized and written for the series. Listeners should not assume any specific line of dialogue is a verbatim historical record unless it is identified as sourced testimony within the episode or show notes.

4. AI-Generated and Synthetic Voice Disclosure

Many voices in this production, including those representing historical figures, witnesses, and secondary participants, are generated using AI voice technology. These synthetic voices are original artistic interpretations and do not attempt to replicate any individual’s actual voice or manner of speech. In many cases, no authentic recording of the individual’s voice exists at all. Their use is solely to bring written historical testimony and dramatized dialogue to life within this scripted production.

5. Authentic Archival Recording Policy

Where genuine historical audio or archival recordings are used in this production, they will be clearly identified as such within the episode or accompanying show notes. Any recording not explicitly identified as archival should be understood to be a dramatized or AI-generated performance created for this series.

6. No Endorsement or Affiliation

This production is an independent work of historical fiction and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any government agency, court, archive, institution, estate, or descendant of any individual depicted. References to real institutions, archives, or historical figures are made for historical context and dramatic purposes only and do not imply any relationship, cooperation, or endorsement.

7. Educational and Entertainment Purpose

This podcast is produced for educational and entertainment purposes. It is not intended as, and should not be relied upon as, a legal, historical, or factual determination of any individual’s guilt or innocence. Views, arguments, and conclusions expressed by fictionalized characters are dramatized for narrative purposes and do not represent findings of fact, legal advice, or the views of the production team.

8. Corrections and Source-Review Policy

We are committed to sourcing accuracy within the constraints of the Rule of 1965. If you believe a specific claim, date, document, or piece of testimony presented in this production is inaccurately sourced or misattributed, please contact us at nicholas.kilburn@thepeoplevsoswald.com with the episode, timestamp, and your concern. Verified corrections will be documented and addressed in show notes or a future episode as appropriate.

9. Copyright and Fair-Use Approach

This production may reference, quote, or adapt material from public records, government documents, and other historical sources believed to be in the public domain or used consistent with fair use principles for the purposes of commentary, criticism, and historical dramatization. If you are a rights holder and believe your material has been used inappropriately, please contact us at nicholas.kilburn@thepeoplevsoswald.com so the matter can be promptly reviewed.

10. Sponsor Disclosure Policy

Some episodes of this production may include paid sponsorship or advertising content. Sponsored content will be clearly identified as such within the episode and presented separately from the dramatized historical narrative. Sponsorship does not influence the historical content, sourcing standards, or editorial conclusions of the series.
